Ticket lifecycle

Every ticket follows the same path. A ticket is only closed after the fix has been verified.

  1. New
  2. Assigned / In progress
  3. Resolved
  4. Closed

Priority levels

Priority comes from business impact and urgency together, not from how annoying the problem is.

PriorityMeaningExample
High Many users affected or a critical function is stopped, with no workaround. Internal website unavailable for everyone.
Medium One team affected, or one user with no workaround. A user cannot open a shared directory.
Low One user, a workaround exists, or the request can be scheduled. New employee equipment request with a start date next week.
The form adds impact and urgency (1 to 3 each): a total of 5-6 is High, 4 is Medium, and 3 or less is Low. A technician may adjust the result once the real situation is understood.

Escalation policy

Escalate when the next person is better placed to fix it, or when waiting makes things worse. Record the reason in the ticket.

Escalate whenTo
A High priority ticket has no progress after 30 minutes.Senior technician / IT lead
The issue needs access or rights the technician does not have.Administrator of that system
Logs suggest a possible compromise, such as a successful login after many failures.Security contact, immediately
The cause is outside company control, such as the internet provider.External provider, with evidence
The employee reports data loss or a lost device.Management and IT lead
Never share passwords with IT staff by chat or email. Technicians will not ask for your password.
